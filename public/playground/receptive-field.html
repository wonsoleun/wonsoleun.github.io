1×1 Conv: same space, mixed channels

A 1×1 Conv never looks at neighboring pixels. It just takes a weighted sum of one pixel's channel vector (R, G, B). In other words, it is the same as applying one FC layer (C_in → C_out) to every pixel. Change the weights and watch the output channels respond.

Saving compute by shrinking channels: Bottleneck

Key takeaways

Receptive field formula

RF = L·(k − 1) + 1 (stride 1)

Stacking L k×k Convs widens the field by (k−1) per layer. Two 3×3 layers see the same region as one 5×5; three see the same as one 7×7.

Why stack small kernels

3×3 × 3 layers: 27C² vs 7×7 × 1 layer: 49C²

You cover the same region with fewer parameters, and the ReLUs between layers add expressive power. That is why VGG and most later CNNs default to 3×3.

Effective receptive field

path count: center ≫ edge

The theoretical region is a square, but central inputs reach the output through far more paths. The deeper the stack, the closer it gets to a Gaussian.

1×1 Conv

RF = 1, params = C_in·C_out

Mixes channels per pixel without looking at space. Used in bottlenecks that shrink and restore channels (ResNet-50, GoogLeNet).
